## What it is

Erythritol is a sugar alcohol, also called a polyol. Despite the name, it is not the alcohol in beer or wine. FDA permits sugar alcohols as sugar substitutes and says they range from 25% to 100% as sweet as sugar. Erythritol is common in "zero sugar" and "keto" products, and is often blended with [stevia](/ingredients/stevia) or [monk fruit](/ingredients/monk-fruit).

## How US labels count it

Sugar alcohols are not counted in Total Sugars or Added Sugars. Under 21 CFR 101.9(c)(6)(iv), a "Sugar alcohol" line on the Nutrition Facts panel is voluntary, but it is required when the label makes a claim about sugar alcohol, total sugars or added sugars and sugar alcohols are present. So a "zero sugar" claim on a product with erythritol should come with a Sugar alcohol line.

## Sugar Depth's verdict: UGLY

Sugar Depth's rules treat erythritol and [xylitol](/ingredients/xylitol) as UGLY (rule U4), the same as artificial sweeteners. Other sugar alcohols, like sorbitol and maltitol, are BAD (rule B1).

This is Sugar Depth's product preference, not a safety finding. FDA permits erythritol. In 2023 FDA reviewed a paper on possible cardiovascular effects of erythritol and said the observational studies it cited did not establish a causal link; FDA said it would keep monitoring.
