## What it is

Sucralose is a high-intensity artificial sweetener. FDA regulates it as a food additive: it was approved for 15 food categories in 1998 and as a general-purpose sweetener in 1999. FDA describes it as about 600 times sweeter than table sugar and heat stable, so it turns up in baked goods as well as drinks. It is sold as Splenda.

## How US labels count it

Sucralose is not a sugar, so it adds nothing to the Total Sugars or Added Sugars lines of the Nutrition Facts panel. A drink sweetened only with sucralose can honestly print **0 g Added Sugars**. The only place you will see it is the ingredient list.

## Sugar Depth's verdict: BAD

Under Sugar Depth's rules, any artificial sweetener makes a label BAD{/* rule X3 */}, however small the amount and wherever it sits in the list. That is a product preference about sweeteners, not a safety finding: FDA permits sucralose in food under its conditions of use.

It is often paired with [acesulfame K](/ingredients/acesulfame-k) in diet drinks. If both are listed, the verdict is still one word: BAD.
