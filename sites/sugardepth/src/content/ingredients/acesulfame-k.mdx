## What it is

Acesulfame potassium is an artificial sweetener that FDA regulates as a food additive. It was approved for specific foods and drinks in 1988 and as a general-purpose sweetener and flavor enhancer in 2003. FDA describes it as about 200 times sweeter than table sugar and heat stable. It is often combined with other sweeteners. Brand names include Sunett and Sweet One.

## How US labels count it

It is not a sugar, so it is not part of Total Sugars or Added Sugars. You find it only in the ingredient list, under one of the names above.

## Sugar Depth's verdict: UGLY

Any artificial sweetener makes a label UGLY (rule U3). When acesulfame K shares a list with [sucralose](/ingredients/sucralose) or [aspartame](/ingredients/aspartame), there is nothing to add up: one artificial sweetener is enough. This is a product preference, not a safety finding.
