## What it is

Aspartame is an artificial sweetener that FDA regulates as a food additive. FDA first allowed it in 1974 for tabletop use and some food categories, and approved it as a general-purpose sweetener in 1996. FDA describes it as about 200 times sweeter than table sugar. It is sold under brand names including NutraSweet, Equal and Sugar Twin. It is not heat stable, so it is rarely used in baked goods.

## The label statement you may see

People with phenylketonuria (PKU) should avoid or restrict aspartame, so FDA requires labels of foods containing it to say the product contains **phenylalanine**. If you see that line, aspartame is in the list.

## How US labels count it

Aspartame is not counted in Total Sugars or Added Sugars. A diet soda can print 0 g sugar and still list aspartame.

## Sugar Depth's verdict: BAD

Any artificial sweetener makes a label BAD under Sugar Depth's rules (rule X3). So does [aspartame-acesulfame salt](/ingredients/aspartame-acesulfame-salt) (E962), a sweetener made from aspartame and [acesulfame K](/ingredients/acesulfame-k). This is Sugar Depth's preference about sweeteners, not a medical finding.
