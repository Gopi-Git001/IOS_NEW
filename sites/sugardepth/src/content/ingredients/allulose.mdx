## What it is

Allulose (also called psicose) is a rare sugar: chemically a sugar, found in small amounts in some foods, and now made for use as a sweetener.

## How US labels count it

In its October 2020 guidance, FDA said it intends to use enforcement discretion so manufacturers can leave allulose out of the **Total Sugars** and **Added Sugars** lines, and can count it at 0.4 calories per gram, pending future rulemaking. Allulose must still be counted in Total Carbohydrate and declared in the ingredient list.

That is why a product sweetened with allulose can show 0 g Added Sugars. Other rare sugars, tagatose and isomaltulose, did not get this treatment and are still counted as sugar ([Federal Register, 2020](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/10/19/2020-22900/sugars-that-are-metabolized-differently-than-traditional-sugars)). Sugar Depth calls those two UGLY.

## Sugar Depth's verdict: BAD

Allulose is BAD under Sugar Depth's rules (rule B3), the same tier as [stevia](/ingredients/stevia) and [monk fruit](/ingredients/monk-fruit). It is not an added sugar on the label, but it is there to sweeten. This is a product preference, not a health claim.
